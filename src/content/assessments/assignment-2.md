## The brief

> Pick one deployed suggestion feature. Run the same inputs through it, and
> at least one comparable alternative, and say honestly what you find.

This is the method from the week 3, 6, and 9 labs, done properly: a fixed
list of inputs, recorded systematically, compared across more than one
system so a finding is a pattern rather than one screenshot. Pick a feature
from the second half of the course so far — a smart-reply system, a
conversational model, an image-completion tool, or a forecast interface —
and audit it the way those labs audited their smaller targets, at
assignment depth rather than lab depth.

"Depth" means: enough inputs that a single odd result doesn't drive your
conclusion, a comparison system so your findings are about the feature and
not about interfaces in general, and a clearly argued answer to the
question every audit in this course eventually asks — given what the
feature actually does, whose interest does it appear to be designed around?

This assignment is also the seed of your final project. Choosing a feature
you're willing to keep working with for another three weeks is worth more
than choosing the flashiest one.

## What you submit

- your input list and raw results, as an appendix or linked spreadsheet
- a written audit, 1,200–1,800 words, structured around your findings rather
  than around a feature-by-feature walkthrough
- one paragraph naming what you'd need to do to turn this into the final
  project's fuller audit
