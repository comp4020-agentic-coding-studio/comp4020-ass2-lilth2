## The brief

> Take the feature you audited for Assignment 2, and produce the audit you
> didn't have time to write the first time.

The final project is not a second, different audit — it's Assignment 2 done
at full depth, on the same feature, informed by everything the second half
of the semester added: the forecasting week's questions about hidden
uncertainty, the co-writing week's questions about disclosure, and week
12's framework for asking whose interest a suggestion actually serves. A
project that switches features at this stage is choosing to start over with
three weeks less time than Assignment 2 had, which rarely goes well.

"Full depth" means the audit could be read by someone with no connection to
this course and land as a complete, convincing case study — not a course
assignment that happens to be long. It should end somewhere Assignment 2
didn't: a specific proposed change to the feature's design, argued from the
evidence you gathered, not a general wish that the feature were more
transparent.

## What you submit

- the complete audit, as a standalone document — expect somewhere in the
  region of 2,500–3,500 words, though the marking is holistic and a shorter
  piece that earns its length is not penalised for being shorter
- your evidence base (input lists, screenshots, logs) as an appendix
- a short note, no more than 150 words, on what changed between your
  Assignment 2 audit and this one, and why
