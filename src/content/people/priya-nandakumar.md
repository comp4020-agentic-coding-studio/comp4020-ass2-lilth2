Priya built input-method software for a mobile keyboard vendor for six years
before this course existed — first candidate-ranking for a Latin-script
predictive keyboard, later the transliteration layer for an Indic-script IME.
She left industry with a specific complaint: nobody she worked with had a
word for the thing they were actually building, which was not "autocomplete"
in the singular sense the press used, but a family of related bets — next-key,
next-word, next-candidate, next-reply — each with its own failure mode and
its own history. Autocomplete Studies is the course she wished existed when
she was trying to explain her job to her family.

She teaches the opening and closing lectures and the weeks that touch
constrained or scripted prediction (T9, IME, forecasting), and reads every
lab's prep note before the lab, not after.
