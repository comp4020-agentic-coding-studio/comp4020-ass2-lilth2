## Before the lab

Nothing to bring. The simulation runs in the browser: a small on-screen
keyboard that scans through rows and columns on a timer, selected with a
single key, with a five-word prediction bar you can also select from.

## In the lab

You'll compose short, assigned sentences twice — once using only the
letter grid, once allowed to use the prediction bar — and log the selection
count for each. Then you'll do it again with a prediction list quietly
swapped for one trained on a mismatched vocabulary, so the words it
predicts are confidently wrong for what you're being asked to say.

The debrief is the point of the lab: most people find the mismatched
predictor more frustrating than no predictor at all, because a wrong-but-
confident suggestion costs a selection to dismiss, on top of the selections
it was supposed to save.

## Afterwards

Nothing here is separately assessed, but if your Assignment 1 build touches
accessible input at all, the selection counts you logged today are the kind
of evidence a marker will expect to see cited, not asserted.
