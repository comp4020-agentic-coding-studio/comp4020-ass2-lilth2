## Before the lab

Nothing to bring. You'll work from a shared set of conversation openings,
each with two possible continuations already prepared — one written by a
person asked to continue it naturally, one generated by a model given the
same opening.

## In the lab

Work through the set in pairs, recording a guess and a one-line reason
before checking the answer — the reason matters more than the guess, since
"it felt off" isn't evidence and "it hedged on a claim a person would have
just stated" is. Some pairs finish confidently wrong on several in a row;
that's the expected result on at least one or two of the set, not a sign
you did the lab badly.

The debrief compares which features people cited across the room, and
whether those features actually correlated with the right answer or just
felt diagnostic in the moment.

## Afterwards

Keep your hit rate and your reasoning notes. Week 12's synthesis lecture
comes back to this exercise directly, and it's a stronger argument with your
own numbers in it than with a general claim about how good models have
gotten.
