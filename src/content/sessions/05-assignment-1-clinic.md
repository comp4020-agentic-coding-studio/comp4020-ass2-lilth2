## Before the lab

Bring whatever state Assignment 1 is in, running. There is no lecture
content this week — the room is work time, cut into short one-on-one slots.

## In the lab

Book a slot when you arrive. Each check-in starts the same way: show your
worst input, the one your build handles least gracefully, before anything
else. That ordering is deliberate — a build's edge cases are more diagnostic
of what it actually does than its best-case demo, and clinic time is short
enough that starting with the interesting part matters.

Outside your own slot, work on the submission. Two students comparing notes
on the same failure mode, independently, is common and fine; two students
with the same code is not, and it tends to be obvious in the check-in when
neither can explain the part that's supposedly theirs.

## Afterwards

Assignment 1 is due at the end of this week — see the [assessment
page](/assessments/) for the exact time and what's marked. There is no
extension built into next week's content for late submissions; if you need
one, ask before the deadline, not after it.
