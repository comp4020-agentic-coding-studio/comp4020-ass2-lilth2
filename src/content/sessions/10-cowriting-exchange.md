## Before the lab

Pick a short-form writing task you'd be comfortable sharing — a scene, a
short opinion piece, an email you'd actually send. You'll need access to a
predictive writing tool that shows its suggestions inline rather than
generating a whole draft at once.

## In the lab

Write with the log running: every suggestion offered gets marked accepted,
rejected, or edited-before-accepting, as you go, not reconstructed
afterwards from memory. Reconstructed logs are consistently less honest
than live ones, in ways people don't intend.

Once finished, trade your piece and your log with a partner, but not in
that order — they read the finished piece first and guess your acceptance
rate before seeing the real log, then compare. Most people's finished pieces
give away much less about the editing process than they expect.

## Afterwards

Keep the log. Week 12's closing lecture asks you to bring one specific
suggestion you rejected and say why, and the log is the only reliable
record of what you actually rejected rather than what you remember
rejecting.
