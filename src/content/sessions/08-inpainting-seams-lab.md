## Before the lab

Bring three or four source images you're comfortable editing and sharing
with the group — photos of places or objects work better than photos of
people for this exercise, and are simpler to get consent-free.

## In the lab

Mask a region of each image — a hand, a background object, a section of
sky — and generate a fill. Then swap images with another pair and try to
find the mask boundary without being told where it was. Some fills are
genuinely undetectable at a glance; the interesting data is in which ones
are, and which categories of region resist detection versus which give
themselves away every time.

Second pass: zoom in. A flaw invisible at thumbnail size is often obvious at
full resolution, which is itself worth noting — most of these images are
viewed at thumbnail size in the wild.

## Afterwards

Your category list — the recurring types of tell, not the individual
images — is the useful artefact here. Individual examples date quickly as
the tools improve; a mechanism ("boundaries where a repeating pattern
meets a non-repeating one") tends to stay useful longer.
