## Before the lab

Pick a recommendation feed you can use in a logged-out or fresh-account
state, and a narrow, specific slice of content to chase — not "cooking" but
"one very specific cuisine," not "sport" but "one team's results." A narrow
target makes the shift easier to detect.

## In the lab

Interact only with content matching your slice — watch it fully, like it,
whatever the platform's strongest engagement signal is — and log the feed's
composition every few interactions. Most people find the shift happens
faster than they expected, and that a few interactions in a genuinely
different direction are enough to pull it back.

Discussion compares slices across the room: some topics shift the feed
almost immediately, others resist for much longer, and guessing why is more
useful than confirming it, since none of you have access to the actual
ranking system.

## Afterwards

Nothing here is separately assessed, but this experiment is a fast way to
generate first-hand evidence for anyone whose final project touches
recommendation, and the pace of the shift you measured is a specific,
citable number a general claim about "the algorithm" isn't.
