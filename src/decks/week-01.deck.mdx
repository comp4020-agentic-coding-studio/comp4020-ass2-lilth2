# Autocomplete Studies

Week 1 — before the screen, the keypad

---

## Twelve keys. Twenty-six letters.

Every letter after the first press of a key is a guess.

That guess is predictive text. It always has been.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Multi-tap

Press `4` once for **G**, twice for **H**, three times for **I**.

Slow. Unambiguous. Nobody predicts anything.

---

## T9

Press `4663` once, in order.

The phone looks up every word that mapping could spell:
**good**, **home**, **hood**, **goof**...

...and picks one. That pick is the whole course.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "Text on 9 keys" — the name says exactly what it does,
> and nothing about how strange that is to actually build.

---

## The tension, stated once

- a small, fixed **vocabulary** (the dictionary)
- an ambiguous **signal** (the keypress sequence)
- a **ranking** someone built, resolving the ambiguity

Every week this semester changes one of these three things.

---

## Week 2 changes the vocabulary's confidence

Autocorrect: the same ranking, applied uninvited.

## Week 5 changes the signal

Code completion: the signal is a program, not a word.

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## Week 7 changes the scale

A chat model: the same ranking, one token at a time,
run long enough to look like an answer.

**home** — and you should be able to defend which one comes first.
